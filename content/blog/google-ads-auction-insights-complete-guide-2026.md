**Category:** [Google Ads](/category/google-ads)

If you are running Google Ads and want to understand why competitors keep appearing above you, Auction Insights is one of the most useful reports inside the platform.

It shows which advertisers are appearing in the same auctions as your ads and gives you data to compare your visibility against theirs. More importantly, it can help answer questions such as:

* Which competitors are appearing most often?
* Are competitors ranking above your ads?
* Are you losing visibility because of budget or Ad Rank?
* Are you actually competing against the businesses you expected?
* Is increasing your Google Ads budget really the right solution?

The mistake many advertisers make is treating Auction Insights as a simple competitor leaderboard. It isn't.

The real value comes from connecting the report with your **impression share, bidding strategy, ad quality, search terms, budget and conversion performance**.

In this guide, we'll break down how Google Ads Auction Insights works in 2026 and how you can turn the data into practical optimization decisions.

## What Is Google Ads Auction Insights?

Google Ads Auction Insights is a competitive analysis report that lets advertisers compare their performance with other advertisers participating in the same auctions.

For example, imagine you are running Google Search Ads for:

**"Google Ads agency"**

Your Auction Insights report may show several other domains competing in the same auctions.

![Google Ads Auction Insights dashboard showing competitor performance metrics](/images/google-ads-auction-insights-dashboard.jpg)


You might see:

| Advertiser    | Impression Share | Overlap Rate | Position Above Rate | Outranking Share |
| ------------- | ---------------: | -----------: | ------------------: | ---------------: |
| Your business |              42% |            — |                   — |                — |
| Competitor A  |              51% |          68% |                 34% |              39% |
| Competitor B  |              35% |          44% |                 22% |              58% |
| Competitor C  |              19% |          27% |                 11% |              71% |

This doesn't mean Competitor A is your biggest competitor across the entire market.

It means Competitor A is frequently appearing in the auctions represented by the report you're looking at.

That's an important distinction.

Auction Insights is best used as a **competitive diagnostic tool**, not as a complete competitor research database.

## How to Find Auction Insights in Google Ads

Finding the report is relatively simple.

Depending on the campaign type and level of analysis you want, you can access Auction Insights from your Google Ads account.

For Search campaigns, you can typically review the report at the:

* Campaign level
* Ad group level
* Keyword level

The keyword-level view can be particularly useful when you want to understand competition around high-value search terms.

For example, instead of analyzing your entire campaign, you could look specifically at keywords such as:

**"emergency plumber"**

or

**"B2B Google Ads agency"**

and see which advertisers are appearing alongside you.

Auction Insights is only available when there is sufficient activity for reporting, so you won't necessarily see the report for every low-volume keyword.

## The 6 Google Ads Auction Insights Metrics You Need to Understand

The Search Auction Insights report provides six important metrics.

Let's break them down.

### 1. Impression Share

Impression share is the percentage of impressions you received compared with the estimated number of impressions you were eligible to receive.

For example, if your ads were eligible for approximately 10,000 impressions and received 6,000, your impression share would be around 60%.

A low impression share doesn't automatically mean your campaign is performing badly.

You need to understand **why** you're missing impressions.

Check your Search Lost IS (budget) and Search Lost IS (rank) metrics.

If budget loss is high, budget may be restricting your visibility.

If rank loss is high, your Ad Rank may need improvement.

This is why simply saying "we need 100% impression share" is usually the wrong approach.

You want strong visibility for **valuable, profitable searches**, not necessarily every available auction.

---

### 2. Overlap Rate

Overlap rate tells you how often another advertiser's ad received an impression when your ad also received an impression.

Suppose Competitor A has an overlap rate of 70%.

That means Competitor A appeared in the same auctions as your ad frequently.

This makes overlap rate useful for identifying the advertisers you are actually encountering in the search results.

However, a high overlap rate does not mean the competitor is beating you.

It simply means you're showing up together frequently.

That's why you should analyze overlap rate alongside position above rate and outranking share.

---

### 3. Position Above Rate

Position above rate tells you how often another advertiser's ad appeared in a higher position than yours when both ads appeared on the same page.

For example:

**Position Above Rate = 45%**

This means the competitor appeared above your ad 45% of the time when both ads were shown.

This is one of the most useful metrics for identifying competitors that consistently appear ahead of you.

But don't immediately respond by increasing bids.

Your next step should be determining whether the issue is:

* Ad Rank
* Bid strategy
* Budget
* Ad relevance
* Landing page experience
* Keyword targeting
* Account structure

A higher bid can help in some situations, but it isn't a universal solution.

---

### 4. Top of Page Rate

Top of page rate measures how frequently an ad appeared above the organic search results.

This is different from simply getting an impression.

An ad can appear on the Search results page without appearing at the top.

For businesses that depend heavily on high-intent lead generation, top-of-page visibility can be particularly important for competitive commercial searches.

However, you should still connect this metric to actual business results.

If increasing top-of-page visibility results in significantly higher CPCs without improving qualified leads, the additional visibility may not be worth the cost.

---

### 5. Absolute Top of Page Rate

Absolute top of page rate shows how often an ad appeared as the first paid ad above the organic results.

This is sometimes referred to as the "number one ad position."

It can be tempting to chase this metric aggressively.

But being first doesn't automatically mean being most profitable.

For example, imagine:

**Position 1:** $35 CPC and 5% conversion rate
**Position 2:** $24 CPC and 6% conversion rate

The second position could potentially produce better economics.

Your objective should be **profitable conversions**, not winning every auction.

---

### 6. Outranking Share

Outranking share tells you how often your ad ranked higher than another advertiser's ad, or showed when their ad did not.

This is one of the most useful metrics for competitive analysis.

Suppose you have:

**Outranking share against Competitor A: 72%**

That suggests you are winning against that advertiser frequently in the auctions covered by the report.

If another competitor has an outranking share of only 28%, that competitor may be much harder to beat.

However, don't interpret this as a simple "win percentage."

Auction Insights metrics are calculated in relation to the auctions represented in your report, so context matters.

## Overlap Rate vs. Outranking Share: What's the Difference?

These two metrics are often confused.

The easiest way to remember them is:

**Overlap rate = How often do we meet?**

**Outranking share = How often do I beat them or show when they don't?**

For example:

Competitor A:

* Overlap Rate: 80%
* Outranking Share: 35%

This means you're frequently competing against them, but your relative performance against them isn't particularly strong.

Now imagine:

Competitor B:

* Overlap Rate: 40%
* Outranking Share: 75%

You're not encountering them as frequently, but when you do, your ads are generally in a stronger position.

That distinction can dramatically change how you prioritize competitors.

## How to Use Auction Insights to Find Your Real Competitors

One of the most useful applications of Auction Insights is discovering competitors you didn't know were competing with you.

Start by exporting your Auction Insights report.

Then create a simple spreadsheet with:

* Competitor domain
* Impression share
* Overlap rate
* Position above rate
* Outranking share
* Top-of-page rate
* Date
* Campaign

Next, categorize competitors into three groups:

### High-priority competitors

These competitors have:

* High overlap
* High impression share
* High position-above rate
* Low outranking share

These are the advertisers you should investigate first.

![Google Ads competitor auction analysis](/images/google-ads-competitor-auction-analysis.jpg)


### Emerging competitors

These advertisers may have lower overall impression share but are appearing more frequently over time.

They could indicate increasing competitive pressure.

### Low-impact competitors

These advertisers have limited overlap and little influence on your most important campaigns.

Don't waste excessive time analyzing every domain in the report.

Focus on the competitors that matter to your revenue-generating keywords.

## How to Use Auction Insights for Budget Decisions

Auction Insights becomes particularly useful when combined with impression share data.

Suppose your campaign has:

**Impression Share: 45%**

and

**Search Lost IS (Budget): 30%**

Increasing budget could potentially help you capture more eligible traffic.

Now consider another campaign:

**Impression Share: 45%**

but

**Search Lost IS (Rank): 30%**

In this situation, simply increasing the budget may not solve the problem.

You may need to work on Ad Rank, bidding, relevance, landing page experience or other campaign factors.

This is why you should never make budget decisions using Auction Insights alone.

Look at the complete picture.

## What to Do When a Competitor Is Consistently Above You

If one competitor consistently appears above your ads, don't immediately start bidding more aggressively.

Work through this checklist:

### 1. Check Search Lost IS (rank)

Determine whether Ad Rank is limiting your visibility.

### 2. Review your ads

Look at your headlines, descriptions, offers and calls to action.

Your ads need to closely match the searcher's intent.

### 3. Review landing pages

A strong ad cannot compensate for a weak landing page.

Check:

* Page speed
* Message match
* CTA visibility
* Trust signals
* Mobile experience
* Form experience
* Relevant content

### 4. Review keyword intent

A competitor may be beating you because you're targeting broad or loosely related searches while they have a more tightly aligned structure.

### 5. Review your bidding strategy

Only increase bids when the economics support it.

If your CPC rises but conversion quality doesn't improve, you may simply be paying more for the same traffic.

## Don't Use Auction Insights as a Reason to Copy Competitors

Competitive research is useful.

Copying competitors isn't.

If another advertiser has a higher impression share, don't automatically assume their strategy is better.

They may have:

* A larger budget
* Different conversion goals
* Higher customer lifetime value
* Different geographic targeting
* Different keyword priorities
* A different bidding strategy

Instead, use Auction Insights to identify **patterns**.

Then use your own conversion data to determine what deserves attention.

This is especially important when managing [Google Ads campaigns focused on lead generation](https://scalewithclicks.com/services/google-ads-agency).

The advertiser with the biggest impression share isn't necessarily the advertiser making the most money.

## How Often Should You Check Auction Insights?

For most accounts, checking Auction Insights every day isn't necessary.

A better approach is to review it regularly and compare trends over time.

A practical schedule is:

**Weekly:**
Review major changes in competitive pressure for important campaigns.

**Monthly:**
Compare competitor impression share, overlap and outranking trends.

**After major campaign changes:**
Check whether the competitive landscape changed after changing budgets, bidding strategies, targeting or account structure.

For highly competitive accounts, you may want more frequent monitoring.

The key is consistency.

A single Auction Insights report tells you what happened during a period.

Multiple reports tell you how the competitive landscape is changing.

## Common Google Ads Auction Insights Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Trying to achieve 100% impression share

More visibility isn't always better.

Focus on profitable traffic.

### Mistake 2: Increasing bids whenever a competitor ranks above you

Ranking isn't the only issue.

Investigate Ad Rank, relevance, budget and conversion economics first.

### Mistake 3: Treating impression share as market share

Auction Insights only reflects the auctions represented in the report.

It isn't a complete picture of the entire market.

### Mistake 4: Ignoring search terms

You may think you're competing against one set of advertisers, while your actual traffic comes from a much broader range of searches.

Review the [Google Ads search terms report and keyword data](/blog/complete-google-ads-keyword-match-types-guide-2026) alongside Auction Insights.

### Mistake 5: Optimizing for position instead of conversions

Being number one is not the same thing as being profitable.

Track:

* Leads
* Sales
* Conversion rate
* Cost per conversion
* Conversion value
* ROAS
* Lead quality

Then make decisions.

## A Simple 2026 Auction Insights Workflow

Here is a practical process you can use each month.

**Step 1:** Select your most important Search campaigns.

**Step 2:** Open Auction Insights.

**Step 3:** Export the report.

**Step 4:** Identify the top overlapping competitors.

**Step 5:** Compare position above rate and outranking share.

**Step 6:** Check impression share and Search Lost IS metrics.

**Step 7:** Review changes from the previous period.

**Step 8:** Identify whether the problem is budget, rank, targeting or competitive pressure.

**Step 9:** Make one or two focused campaign changes.

**Step 10:** Measure the impact on conversions and cost efficiency.

This approach is much more useful than looking at the report and simply saying, "Competitor X is beating us."

## Final Thoughts

Google Ads Auction Insights is one of the best tools for understanding competitive pressure inside your Search campaigns.

But the report becomes truly valuable when you stop treating it as a leaderboard.

Use **impression share** to understand your visibility.

Use **overlap rate** to identify who you're competing with most often.

Use **position above rate** to see who is appearing above you.

Use **top-of-page and absolute top-of-page rates** to understand ad visibility.

And use **outranking share** to understand your relative competitive position.

Most importantly, connect these metrics to **budget, Ad Rank, CPC, conversions and profitability**.

Your goal isn't to beat every competitor in every auction.

Your goal is to make sure your Google Ads budget is capturing the searches that can generate the most valuable business results.
